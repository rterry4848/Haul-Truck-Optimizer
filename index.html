Cost per ton bottoms out where truck delivery capacity just meets the paver's max rate. Fewer trucks starve the paver; more trucks just queue and burn hours. Each truck is only credited for full completed cycles in the shift — a truck that starts a load but can't finish it before the shift ends gets no partial credit, so tons/shift can step rather than climb smoothly as trucks are added.

Wave 1's shared start time is timed backward from "Paving start time" — load time plus haul-out — so the first load lands exactly when you want paving to begin. Each later wave starts once the plant limit clears (max stacked × load time). All trucks in the same wave show that one shared "Start" time, but under the hood only one truck can be under the silo at once — each truck's actual loading is staggered by load time within its wave, and that real load moment is what drives its planned finish, loads, and hours. Nobody is paid for the flat shift length, only for cycles actually completed.
